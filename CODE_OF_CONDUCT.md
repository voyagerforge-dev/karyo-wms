# Code of conduct

Karyo is built in the open by the people who use it, run it and change it. This is how we treat
each other here.

## Our standard

Treat everyone here as someone whose time and judgement you depend on.

- Be direct about the work and considerate about the person. Reviewing a change hard is
  respectful; reviewing a person is not.
- Assume the other person had a reason. Ask what it was before rewriting their design, their
  document or their decision.
- Disagree with evidence. "This is wrong" is worth little; "this is wrong, here is the failing
  case" is worth a lot.
- Accept a decision once it is made, and say plainly if you still disagree. Re-arguing a settled
  call in every later review is a conduct problem, not a technical one.
- Credit work accurately. Do not present someone else's change as yours, and do not quietly
  remove their name from it.
- Welcome people who are new. Everyone here once asked the question you now consider obvious.

## Honesty about evidence

Warehouses are configured, deployed and run from what is written here. Misreporting what you did
misleads someone who cannot re-check your work, so it belongs in this document:

- Do not describe a check as passed when it was not run, and do not present a skipped test as a
  green one.
- Do not present a plan, a design or a document as proof that the code behaves that way.
- Say plainly what you did not do, could not do, or chose not to do.
- If you break something, say so early. Nobody is judged for a broken build; people are judged for
  a broken build that someone else had to discover.

## Privacy

Do not post anyone's private information. Do not post credentials, licence tokens, customer
records or a warehouse's real data in an issue, a pull request, a commit or a comment - your own
included. Redact before you paste. A live warehouse is never a test fixture.

## Unacceptable behaviour

Harassment; discriminatory, sexualised or demeaning language or imagery; personal attacks and
sustained hostility; deliberate intimidation; unwelcome personal attention; publishing someone's
private information without their permission; and retaliation against anyone who raises a concern
under this document.

Deliberately falsifying evidence of work is treated as a conduct failure, not as a mistake.

## Scope

This applies in every space the project runs: the repository, its issues, pull requests, commit
messages and review comments, and anywhere someone represents the project. It applies equally to
work carried out by automated tools and agents acting for a person; the person who runs the tool is
accountable for what it does here.

## Raising a concern

Raise it privately with the maintainers named in [CODEOWNERS](CODEOWNERS). This repository's
private channel is GitHub's private reporting form: open the **Security** tab, choose **Report a
vulnerability**, and begin the title with "Conduct:". Only the maintainers can read it. For a
problem with a specific comment, issue or pull request, GitHub's **Report content** option on that
item works as well. If the concern is about a maintainer, use **Report content** and choose to
report to GitHub, which reaches GitHub rather than this project.

Reports are handled privately. Whoever handles one tells you what they intend to do before they do
it, unless that would put someone at risk. Outcomes range from a private word to a temporary or
permanent ban from the project's spaces. Raising a concern in good faith never counts against you,
whatever the outcome turns out to be.

For a security vulnerability rather than a conduct concern, use [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
